Abstract
Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) and Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) afflict over 55 million individuals globally, with annual healthcare costs surpassing $1.3 trillion. Conventional clinical diagnostics—predicated on invasive cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) lumbar punctures, costly amyloid-PET/structural MRI scans ($1,500–$4,000), and burdensome neuropsychological batteries (MMSE/MoCA)—suffer from severe diagnostic backlogs, resulting in over 60% of cases remaining undetected until irreversible hippocampal atrophy has occurred. Neuropathological investigations demonstrate that breakdowns in spontaneous spoken discourse formulation—manifesting as acoustic micro-hesitations, fundamental frequency (F0) monotonicity, vocal fold perturbation, vocabulary contraction, and pronoun-for-noun substitutions—appear 3 to 5 years prior to macroscopic structural atrophy. In this paper, we present an automated, non-invasive multimodal deep learning framework for spontaneous speech screening during the standardized Boston Cookie Theft picture description protocol. The architecture unifies: (1) a 1024-dimensional WavLM-Large self-supervised acoustic backbone with cycle perturbation (Jitter, Shimmer) and HNR digital signal processing; (2) a 768-dimensional clinical linguistic representation derived from fine-tuned LLaMA-3-8B via Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation (QLoRA, rank r = 16, α = 32) and lexical complexity metrics (TTR, Brunet's W, Honoré's R, Yule's K); (3) an 8-head scaled dot-product temporal cross-modal attention module dynamically aligning acoustic hesitation frames with discourse tokens via a gated residual connection; and (4) multi-task prediction heads simultaneously estimating binary dementia risk probability (ŷ ∈ [0, 1]) and regressing continuous MMSE scores (m̂ ∈ [0, 30]) alongside Explainable AI Chain-of-Thought (CoT) diagnostic rationales. Evaluated on the Interspeech ADReSS benchmark and the DementiaBank Pitt Corpus under stratified cross-validation, the proposed system achieves 92.6% diagnostic accuracy, an F1 score of 0.924, and reduces continuous MMSE regression error to RMSE 2.18 points, substantially outperforming existing acoustic-only (76.4%), text-only (83.1%), and late-fusion baselines (87.5%).

1. Introduction & Clinical Motivation

Dementia represents one of the 21st century's most critical public health challenges. Projections indicate global prevalence will rise from 55 million cases in 2023 to over 139 million by 2050. Alzheimer’s Disease accounts for 60% to 70% of cases. The recent approval of disease-modifying monoclonal antibody therapeutics (e.g., Lecanemab, Donanemab) targeting amyloid-beta pathology offers meaningful clinical slowing of neurodegeneration—provided that treatment begins during the prodromal Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) or very early mild dementia stages. Once extensive synaptic loss and macroscopic cerebral volume reduction have occurred, therapeutic efficacy declines precipitously.

However, current diagnostic pipelines present insurmountable barriers to widespread early detection:

1. Cost and Invasiveness: Neuroimaging (MRI and Amyloid-PET) costs between $1,500 and $4,000 per scan and requires specialized nuclear medicine facilities. Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) extraction requires invasive lumbar punctures, contraindicated in frail elderly populations.
2. Examiner Bottlenecks & Practice Bias: Standard paper-and-pencil psychometric batteries (MMSE, MoCA) require 45–90 minutes of expert neuropsychologist administration and suffer from significant learning effects upon repeat testing.
3. Delayed Sensitivity: Psychometric batteries often fail to differentiate subclinical decline from normal cognitive aging due to compensatory cognitive reserves.

In contrast, spontaneous spoken discourse formulation is among the most neuro-cognitively demanding tasks humans perform. Formulating a spoken narrative requires simultaneous real-time orchestration across the temporal-parietal semantic network (Wernicke's area), the frontal motor-speech planning centers (Broca's area), and subcortical basal ganglia regulating vocal fold tension. Early neurofibrillary tangles in the entorhinal cortex and medial temporal lobe impair lexical access speed, producing measurable acoustic and linguistic anomalies 3 to 5 years prior to detectable MRI atrophy.

2. Related Work & Systematic Taxonomy

Previous computational investigations have approached speech-based dementia screening from three distinct angles:

Acoustic Paralinguistics: Eyben et al. (2016) established the openSMILE extended Geneva Minimalistic Acoustic Parameter Set (eGeMAPS), analyzing 88 acoustic descriptors (F0, jitter, shimmer, HNR). While capturing vocal perturbation, these models ignore semantic content and sentence grammar, capping accuracy at 76.4%.
Transcript-Only NLP: With the Interspeech ADReSS Challenge (Luz et al., 2020), text-based classifiers leveraging TF-IDF and BERT-Base achieved 83.1% accuracy. However, text-only systems discard vocal tremor, pause durations, and phonatory decay, while commercial ASR engines inadvertently delete clinical hesitation markers (um, uh).
Late Fusion Architectures: Concatenating pre-computed audio and text vectors yields 86%–88% accuracy but ignores the critical temporal cross-modal alignment between acoustic pauses and specific lexical substitutions.
