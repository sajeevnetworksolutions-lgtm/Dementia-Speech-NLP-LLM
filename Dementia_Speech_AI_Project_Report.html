1. Executive Summary & Clinical Rationale

Alzheimer's Disease (AD) and Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) are conventionally identified via costly structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or protracted paper-and-pencil neuropsychological batteries (e.g., MMSE, MoCA). As a result, over 60% of cases are diagnosed at late, irreversible stages. Neurocognitive studies demonstrate that subtle impairments in spontaneous spoken discourse—such as micro-hesitations, reduced pitch modulation, vocabulary contraction, and pronoun-for-noun substitutions—occur 3 to 5 years prior to visible anatomical brain atrophy.

This project delivers a complete, non-invasive multimodal screening system based on the standardized Boston Cookie Theft picture description protocol. The architecture combines self-supervised acoustic representations (WavLM-Large 1024d) and clinical language models (LLaMA-3 QLoRA 768d) through a temporal cross-modal attention layer (512d) into multi-task prediction heads that estimate both cognitive risk percentage and continuous MMSE scores (0–30) with Explainable AI Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales.
